OptioHire vs. generic ATS platforms: what's actually different

14 August 2026 · 7 min read · OptioHire Team

Most hiring software is built to store applications. That is useful. It is also not the same job as deciding who should move forward. “Applicant tracking” and “hiring decision support” get sold as one category. They are not.

A generic ATS is a filing system with workflow around it: receive CVs, park them in a pipeline, hope a human still has time to read. Keyword filters take the first pass. Manual review still does the actual ranking. Status updates, when they happen, are extra work. Reasoning lives in someone’s head, or in a Slack thread that the next coordinator never sees.

Tracking where a CV sat is not the same as explaining why a person advanced.

What actually differs

Generic ATSOptioHire
First passKeyword filteringRequirement-based scoring
Review loadManual review still required to make a shortlistRanked shortlist with visible reasoning
Why this person?No scoring reasoning on the recordTransparent reasoning against the role
Candidate communicationStatus updates are manual, so silence is commonAutomated outcome updates from the same workflow
After the factHard to reconstruct the decisionFull audit trail

Where a generic ATS still makes sense

If you hire a handful of people a year, for highly niche roles where one hiring manager will read every file anyway, a lightweight tracker can be enough. The pile is small. The decision is already sitting in one person’s head. Buying decision infrastructure for that volume is optional.

The gap shows up when volume arrives: campus intakes, retail turnover, engineering roles with two hundred applicants, or more than one department sharing a process. At that point keyword theatre and unread PDFs are not a tooling preference. They are the process.

What to do next

If you need a system that scores fairly, explains itself, and tells candidates what happened, start a free trial and run one live role through it. That is the only comparison that matters.
